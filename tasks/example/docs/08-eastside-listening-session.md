# Eastside Listening Session Notes

Wednesday, September 23, 2026, 6:30–8:00 pm, Cedar Park Community Center, Room B.
Facilitator and notes: Rosa Delgado, Eastside Branch Manager. Spanish interpretation: Nilda Ortiz (volunteer).

Attendance: 26 adults and 9 children. Trustee Marjorie Fleck came and listened.

The Eastside Branch is open Tuesday to Friday 12–6 and Saturday 10–2. I opened by explaining that the trustees are discussing longer evening hours at the Main Library and want to hear from Eastside residents before they decide. I asked two questions: when would you use the library, and what gets in the way?

## What we heard

**Saturdays.** This came up first and most often. For many people Saturday is the only day off, and the branch closes at 2. Several parents said they come on Saturday morning with the kids and get told to pack up just as the kids settle in. At least a dozen people asked for the branch to stay open until 5 on Saturdays.

**Getting to Harlow Street.** Most people who spoke don't drive, or share one car in the household. The Route 14 bus runs between Cedar Park and downtown every 40 minutes, and the last Route 14 back to Eastside leaves Harlow Street at 7:40 pm on weekdays. There is no Sunday service. One man said: "If Main is open till nine, I still have to leave at half past seven or walk home two miles." Two women said they would use Main in the evening if they had a ride.

**Work schedules.** A good number of people work second shift at the Ashby Foods plant (3 to 11:30 pm) or at the distribution centers out on Route 9. For them, evenings don't help at all. "At six o'clock I'm on the line." Mornings and Saturdays are what work for them.

**Computers.** The branch has six public computers and there's a wait most afternoons. Parents said their kids need them for homework, and many households have only a phone. Two people asked whether we could lend laptops.

**Teenagers downtown.** Several parents said their teenagers take the bus to Main after school because it has the teen room and more computers. Opinions on later hours there were mixed. One mother said, "Right now he's home by 6:30. If it's open until nine, he'll stay until nine and then call me for a ride I can't give him." Another said she would rather her daughter be at the library than anywhere else.

**Evening hours at Main.** When I asked directly, a handful of people (I counted six) said later hours at Main would help them. Most of those six have cars.

## Follow-up

Trustee Fleck said she would bring the Saturday request to the other trustees. I have asked Denise to include a cost for longer Eastside Saturday hours in her options memo. Nineteen attendees left contact details on the sign-in sheet and asked to hear what the trustees decide.
