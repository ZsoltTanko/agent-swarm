# Radio Interview Transcript: Trustee Gregory Tan on Evening Hours

"Town Square," Millbrook Community Radio, Thursday, October 8, 2026. Host: Dana Whitcomb. Guest: Gregory Tan, vice-chair of the Millbrook Public Library trustees. Transcribed by station volunteers and lightly edited for length.

DANA WHITCOMB: Greg, the library trustees vote next month on longer evening hours. Where do you stand?

GREGORY TAN: I want Monday through Thursday until nine. And this isn't my pet project. The Friends did a survey this summer, six hundred-some people, and fifty-eight percent said they'd come more often if we stayed open later. That's a mandate.

WHITCOMB: Why nine and not, say, eight?

TAN: Because if you're going to do it, do it properly. Somebody who gets off work at five-thirty, picks up the kids, makes dinner, they're not getting to Harlow Street by seven. Look at Fairhaven. They went to nine o'clock four nights a week and their circulation went up twelve percent.

WHITCOMB: What does it cost?

TAN: Director Okafor has the numbers. But here's what people don't realize: the state gives a library more aid once it's open more hours. For us that's about thirty thousand dollars a year. So this basically pays for itself.

WHITCOMB: Some people have raised safety. It's dark by five in the winter.

TAN: I asked Sergeant Ortega about that directly. He told me there were four calls for service within a block of the library between six and ten p.m. in the whole of last year. Four. It's one of the quietest blocks downtown. I understand the concern, but the numbers don't support it.

WHITCOMB: And the staff? I've heard not everyone is keen.

TAN: Change is hard, and I respect our staff enormously. But the Commons coffee shop across the street is full of students with laptops until ten every night. The demand is there. If we need to hire, we hire. Other libraries manage it.

WHITCOMB: Who do you think benefits most?

TAN: Working families, especially on the Eastside. Parents working second jobs, the ones who can't get to us before six. When I think about who we're failing with a six o'clock closing, I think about them.

WHITCOMB: We have a caller. Linda, from Birch Hill, go ahead.

CALLER (LINDA): Hi. I love the library, but I'm a nurse and I work twelve-hour shifts. Eight or nine at night doesn't help me. Why not Sunday afternoons? That's when my family could actually come.

TAN: It's a fair question, Linda. Sundays are harder. Under the staff contract, Sunday work is double time, so it costs a lot more per hour. Evenings get us more for the money.

WHITCOMB: Last word, Greg?

TAN: The library belongs to everyone, and right now it closes before half the town gets off work. We can fix that. Come to the trustees' meeting on the seventeenth and tell us what you think.
