**From:** Hal Brennan
**Date:** Tuesday, September 15, 2026, 4:47 PM

Denise,

It could. The one Tier II requirement you don't meet today is hours: the central library must be open at least 52 hours a week on its posted schedule. You already meet the other Tier II standards (certified director, local support per capita, materials spending). A few details that trip people up:

- Only the central library counts. Branch hours don't add to the 52.
- The 52 hours must be the regular posted schedule year-round. A summer reduction of up to 8 weeks is allowed.
- Tier status is set from your annual report, due March 31. We certify the schedule in effect on that date, as long as it's expected to continue.
- Aid at the new rate is paid the following fiscal year, in October and April installments.

For your population band (20,000–29,999), Tier I is $1.10 per capita and Tier II is $2.45. On your certified population of 23,400, that's about $31,600 a year more.

One caution: if a Tier II library drops below 52 hours for more than 8 weeks during the aid year, we recertify it at Tier I and recover the difference, prorated.

Hal

Denise, Hal,

Two things from the town side.

First, there's no mid-year supplemental appropriation available. The Council adopted FY27 under the 2.5% cap and has turned down every mid-year request so far. A pilot would have to be paid for inside the library's existing budget.

Second, be careful about treating the state money as new money. When your aid went up in 2019, the Council cut the town's appropriation to the library by the same amount. Their view was that the state was picking up part of the bill. I'd argue that aid earned by adding hours should follow the hours, and I'll say so in the FY28 budget process, but I can't promise how the Council will see it.

Lorraine

**From:** Hal Brennan
**Date:** Friday, September 18, 2026, 10:15 AM

Right on both counts. The aid year would be July 1, 2027 to June 30, 2028. If your trustees ended the pilot in June 2027, the certification would stand, but you'd be under 52 hours during the aid year and we'd recover the difference. In practice, a pilot that counts toward Tier II is an eighteen-month commitment.

Hal
